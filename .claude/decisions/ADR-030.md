# ADR-030 — The visitor reads the answer, in one language; the adopter reads the "why"

- **Status:** Accepted — 2026-09-25. Proposed 2026-09-23: the product owner asked for it ("remove the why in the AI
  answers, no user will care for those. And make the output cleaner, less is more here. Be direct and
  objective. Only display one language on the answers (language used in website/webapp pref)")
- **Date:** 2026-09-23
- **Deciders:** Maintainers
- **Amends:** [ADR-012](./ADR-012.md) (the "why?" affordance over a real record), [ADR-017](./ADR-017.md)
  (model disclosure *in "Why?"*), [ADR-019](./ADR-019.md) (removal count *in "Why?"*). None is superseded:
  Rule 9 and the disclosure of a model both stand; what changes is where they are shown.
- **Relates to:** [ADR-026](./ADR-026.md) (one question, never a menu); Rule 9, Rule 14.

## Context

Screenshots of the bench on 2026-09-23 showed three things a visitor should not have to read.

1. **Mechanics under every answer.** Each answer carried a "Why?" disclosure (stage, latency, the
   candidates and their scores, "with no model call") and a Copy button. Nobody asking "where do I pay
   fees" wants a cosine score; the product owner: "no user will care for those".
2. **Filler.** A move read "Opening Calendar" *and* the screen's stock description. A clarifying
   question ended "Not what you meant? Tell me a bit more about what you want to do." Small talk came
   with a second sentence. Choice cards carried up to three lines of description.
3. **Two languages in one answer.** The widget's words follow the page (`<html lang>`), but a
   registry holds the app's words in one language — its first. On the English page of a Portuguese app
   (one that ships pt-PT and en) the answer read "Do you mean Painel, or Avaliações?" over two Portuguese
   descriptions. The generator already read every language the app ships (`languages`) and used the
   others only for matching examples; the English names were known at build time and thrown away.
   The same answer also mixed in `description` (English, machine-written for retrieval) when a screen had
   no explanation.

## Decision

We will show a visitor the answer, only, in one language — the page's — and give the adopter the record.

**The "why" goes to the adopter.** No "Why?" under any answer. Every routed turn's `TurnTrace` is
announced on `window` as `nextgen:turn` (`{ trace }`). Rule 9 is unchanged: the record exists for every
routed turn and "why" is answered from it, never from model narration. It is read by the adopter (logs,
their own staff tooling, the bench) instead of the visitor. The one mechanic a visitor is still shown is
ADR-017's disclosure: "Answered with help from a model." under a turn a model decided (`decidedAt: 'llm'`)
or wrote (`prose`). ADR-019's count of removed sentences is no longer shown. The removals are in the
record.

**Less is more.**
- A move says "Opening X", nothing else. A model's validated answer (ADR-019) is still its body: it is an
  answer, not a description.
- "You're on X" says nothing more. The screen is behind the panel.
- Questions are short: "Looking for X?", "X or Y?", "Open X?". Each choice has one line (≤ 90 characters,
  truncated with an ellipsis on screen).
- Hints are gone, except one: a refusal that points nowhere ends with one short line ("Ask me where to find
  something else."), so it is never a dead end.
- Copy stays only on an answer from the app's own assistant (ADR-026). Those words may be worth keeping;
  "Opening Calendar" is not.
- `description` is never shown to a visitor.

**One language.**
- A registry file says which language its words are in: `language`, the app's first. Each capability
  carries `translations` for the app's other languages: `{ "en": { "title", "explanation", "refusal" } }`.
  The generator writes them from the same translation files it already reads. A title in each language
  comes from the app's own label. An explanation is written only from what the page says *in that
  language* (its i18n keys, never literal headings). If a language has nothing of its own to say, the
  explanation is absent for it.
- The widget picks one display language: the page's, if the registry has it; else English if the app ships
  it; else the registry's own. Its own words and the app's words are both shown in it. A translation that
  lacks an explanation or a refusal shows none, never another language's. The only exception is a name,
  which is a name in any language.
- Translations are display-only: never embedded, never matched. The router keeps the registry it was given
  (`inLanguage` builds a separate view for presentation), so no score, threshold or golden decision moves.
- The widget follows `<html lang>` live (a `MutationObserver`). An app that switches language without
  touching it calls `handle.setLanguage(tag)`. Guided steps are written in the first language only, so in
  another language they are not offered.

## Alternatives considered

**Do nothing.** "Why?" is the product's most distinctive promise (explainable routing), so there was a
case for keeping it. Rejected by the product owner, and on the evidence: the visitor is not the audience for
scores. Moving the record to an event keeps the promise for the audience it serves.

**Keep "Why?" behind a setting, off by default.** It is cheap to build, but it is a second UI to keep
accessible and translated for a reader who does not exist. An adopter who wants a visible "why" can build
one from `nextgen:turn` in their own design, for their staff.

**Translate at run time.** Machine translation in the browser is a model and a download, and in the cloud
it is a network call (Rule 8). Rejected. The app's own translation files already have the words.

**Ship one registry per language.** This is simpler for the widget. But it doubles what is fetched and
cached, and it doubles the files Rule 17's serving logic has to get right. The indexes would also have to
be computed per language, or the router would score one language against another's vectors. Rejected:
translations beside the words keep one registry, one index and one calibration.

**Show the primary language when a translation is missing.** This is what happens today. It is the
defect.

**Keep the stock description under "Opening X".** It was written to help. But the visitor is about to see
the screen itself, and the description was the longest thing in the answer.

## Consequences

- **The visitor loses the ability to see why.** A curious or sceptical visitor has no way to inspect the
  decision. Explainability is now a promise to the adopter, not to the end user. Wherever the docs say
  otherwise, they are wrong.
- **ADR-019's removal disclosure is weaker.** The visitor is no longer told that part of a model's answer
  was withheld. It is in the record only.
- **A page in a third language falls back to English or the first language.** Spanish visitors of a pt/en
  app read English. The widget itself speaks only English and Portuguese.
- **Adopter-written text is single-language.** `knowledge.json` explanations, limits and phrases are in
  the language they were written in. On the other language's page, a declared limit shows the generic
  "I can take you to any screen in this app", and a screen's knowledge-written explanation is replaced by
  the derived one (or none). A per-language `knowledge.json` is not built.
- **Explanations in the other languages are thinner.** They come only from translated copy, so many
  screens have a name and no explanation in English. That is by design (nothing mixed, nothing invented),
  but choice cards show a bare name more often.
- **`<html lang>` must be right.** An app that sets it wrongly, or never, gets the widget in the wrong
  language unless it passes `lang` or calls `setLanguage`.
- **A registry written before this ADR carries no `language`.** The widget then assumes the page's
  language, which is the old behaviour, mix included, until the adopter rebuilds.
- **`nextgen:turn` puts the trace on the page.** Anything on the page can read it: the visitor's
  question and the candidates their role could reach. Nothing in it is new to the page (it is the
  visitor's own words and their own role's registry), but an adopter who forwards it to analytics is
  shipping visitors' questions to a third party. That is their call, and it is documented in AGENTS.md.
