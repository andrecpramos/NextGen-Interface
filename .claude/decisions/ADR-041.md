# ADR-041 — No wrong move without a model, and a request to change something is offered its screen, never followed

- **Status:** Accepted — by the owner, in session, 2026-09-25. Proposed the same day: the owner decided
  both rules in session ("Zero, gated"; "Say so, then offer", split by verb; "Adopt it" for the
  shared-words rule; fam-0062 accepts both screens)
- **Date:** 2026-09-25
- **Deciders:** Andre, Architect
- **Creates:** Rules 19 and 20 in PLAN.md §2.
- **Extends:** [ADR-025](./ADR-025.md) (a limit about acting refuses only a request to act) to every
  request to change something, declared limit or not. **Bounds:** [ADR-031](./ADR-031.md) — only a request
  to *start* something moves and opens a form. **Corrects:** [ADR-032](./ADR-032.md)'s measurement, which
  left out wrong moves.

## Context

**Wrong moves.** Model-free, the router moves a visitor only when stage 1 or stage 2 is sure, and until
2026-09-23 the family fixture had one wrong move in 69 turns. ADR-032's scoring raised right-screen moves
from 39 to 45 of 55, and its table reported that. It did not report that wrong moves rose from 1 to 4, and
out-of-scope requests executed from 0 to 1. The golden gate (`evaluate-golden.mjs --check`) treats every
changed decision alike, so regenerating the scorecard in the same commit passed it. Three of the four went
to notification *settings* for "tenho avisos por ler" (unread notices), because the fixture's knowledge
declares "avisos" on both the settings screen and the inbox. The scores were confident (margin 0.10–0.35)
and wrong. Recalibrating could not remove them: the thresholds were already swept at 100% precision on the
calibration split, and all five were in the held-out split.

**Requests to change something.** The first production integration found users asking the orb to *do*
things: "cancel tomorrow's session", "delete my son's medical record". The router treated these as
navigation. With stage 3 on (ADR-036), qwen3:14b sent "apaga a ficha médica do meu filho" straight to the
medical screen, as if the deleting were done. The model-free path only asked a question about it.
`isActionRequest` recognised the verb, but nothing acted on that unless the app had declared an acting
limit (ADR-025).

## Decision

**Rule 19 — without a model, the router never moves a visitor to a wrong screen or follows an
out-of-scope request.**

1. **Stage 2 does not accept when every content word tying the question to the top screen is also the
   runner-up's** (`sharedWithRunnerUp` in `stages.ts`). The words are the question's content words
   (function words and question frames are not evidence), stemmed like the declared words, against each
   screen's whole document. When they are shared, the scores are not evidence for one screen over the
   other. The turn is asked, with the likely one first (one tap), or decided by stage 3 when it is on. No
   number is involved, so Rule 14 is untouched. The trace says why: "not moved: … belong to the runner-up
   too".
2. **The golden gate fails on any wrong move**, in-scope to a wrong screen or out-of-scope executed,
   before and independently of the scorecard comparison. A regenerated scorecard cannot accept one.
3. A golden question the owner judges genuinely ambiguous in the app may name more than one right screen
   (`expect.alsoAccept`), recorded with the decision. fam-0062 ("onde mudo o meu endereço de email") accepts
   /settings as well as /settings/notifications: the settings screen's declared purpose names the email
   address.

**Rule 20 — a request to change something is offered its screen, never followed there.**

4. `isChangeRequest(utterance)`: the utterance carries an action concept other than the three that *start*
   something (book, create, fill), and is not a "where" or "how do I" question. Words that are as often
   nouns (email, message, call, post, order, change, update, transfer, process, print) count only as a
   command: first, or after "please", "you" or "to". So "avisos por email" and "my orders" name things.
5. In the cascade, a turn that would execute on a `navigate` or `read` capability for a change request
   ends `out_of_scope`, with `capabilityId` naming that screen and the note "offered, not opened (Rule 20)".
   Every stage passes through `settle()`, so every stage is covered. `write` and `destructive`
   capabilities keep their confirmation path (ADR-004, dormant until WP-48).
6. The widget answers "I can't do that for you. Try here:" with the screen as a link. When the visitor is
   already on it, it says "It's handled on this screen." When the router was unsure, it asks the same way.
   It never moves, whatever `decide` says.
7. A request to **start** something ("quero marcar um treino", "add a player") still moves and opens the
   form (ADR-031): the visitor does the task there.

## Measured

2026-09-25, family fixture, hashing, model-free (`evaluate-golden.mjs`):

| | Before (ADR-032) | After |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong moves (in-scope to wrong screen + out-of-scope executed) | **4 + 1** | **0 + 0** |
| Right screen, moved or offered (top-1) | 45/55 (81.8%) | 39/55 (70.9%) |
| Out-of-scope refused | 71.4% | 71.4% |
| In-scope wrongly refused | 0 | 0 |

Stage-2 accepts, before the golden gate's other rules: 31 right and 5 wrong became 25 right and 1 wrong.
The 1 was fam-0062, settled by item 3. The 6 right moves given up ("resumo do plantel" → Squad,
"notificações por ler" → Notifications) are now "You probably want Squad?", one tap, or a model call with
stage 3 on.

Tried and rejected: accepting only when the whole-document score and the nearest-phrasing score name the
same leader. It removed none of the five, because both signals agreed on the wrong screen, and it cost 7
points of top-1.

## Why not the alternatives

- **Do nothing.** Five confident wrong moves on a 69-turn fixture, and a gate that waves the next one
  through with the commit that causes it.
- **Record the five as known and gate only new ones.** The owner chose zero. A known wrong move is still
  a visitor sent to the wrong screen.
- **Raise `tauHigh` by hand.** Rule 14. The wrong moves scored 0.31–0.42, among the right ones.
- **Fix the fixture's knowledge.** It would move the number without changing the product, which is the
  demo tuning the owner ruled out (STATUS row 15). An adopter's own interview is where declarations get
  better.
- **Treat every request to act alike** (start and change). "Schedule a session" would lose its
  form-opening move, which the first integration found users valued most.

## The reversibility test

Two policies in code (`stages.ts`, `cascade.ts`), a detector (`action.ts`), a presenter branch and a gate.
Adopters see the change as fewer moves and a new answer. It is reversible, but it is a policy table and a
numbered rule, so an ADR is owed.

## Consequences

- **Fewer instant moves.** 6 of 55 fixture questions that moved correctly now take one tap. On an app whose
  screens share much vocabulary, the share asked could be higher. Stage 3 recovers them when it is on.
- **A change request takes one more tap than before** when it used to land on the right screen ("mudar o
  email das notificações" was a move; it is now "I can't do that for you. Try here: Notification
  settings").
- **The change-verb list is closed and bilingual** (EN/PT), and a third language gets none of this. The
  noun-or-verb exceptions are a heuristic, tested on 19 phrasings, not on real visitors.
- The shared-words rule depends on what screens declare. Two screens that both declare a word the visitor
  uses will always be asked about. That is the intent, and it rewards distinct declarations.

## Compliance

- `no-wrong-moves.test.ts` (core-intent): the change/start/where split, both Rule 19 cases, and Rule 20 on
  the cascade.
- `present.test.ts` ("a request to change something"): link, not move; already here; unsure; never under
  `eager`.
- `evaluate-golden.mjs --check` (CI): fails on any wrong move, model-free.
- The widget cannot tell whether a screen *really* lets the visitor make the change: "Try here" is the
  honest wording for that, a **convention** held by the word table.
