# ADR-042 — The assistant knows what each screen lets a visitor do, and who may; a limit never contradicts the app

- **Status:** Accepted — 2026-10-15. Proposed the same day: the product owner asked for it after a real
  integration, where the assistant could not tell what a screen lets a visitor do, and refused requests
  the app supports.
- **Date:** 2026-10-15
- **Deciders:** Maintainers
- **Amends:** [ADR-041](./ADR-041.md) item 6 (the words a change request is answered with), and the generic
  catalogue of [ADR-009](./ADR-009.md) / [ADR-025](./ADR-025.md). **Extends:** [ADR-031](./ADR-031.md)
  (controls that open something) to controls that do something. **Applies** Rule 17 to controls, and Rule
  19's shared-words reading to refusals.

## Context

A real app (130 screens, 10 roles) was measured on 67 indirect, in-scope questions its roles would ask,
written before any change (2026-10-15, qwen3:14b stage 3): **45 right, 16 moved to a wrong screen, 5 told
the app could not do it.** Four causes, all in what the generator emitted:

1. **A screen was known by its labels only.** "Classes · Overview — part of the classes area, at
   /classes." What a screen lets a visitor *do* was nowhere: only the controls that open a dialog
   (ADR-031) were read, and only in the page's own file. "Cancelar aula", "Publicar", "Aprovar" live in
   the toolbars, rows and sheets a page renders.
2. **The generic limits contradicted the app.** "Acting outside this application — sending an email or a
   message" was declared for every app; there, a request to email a group's parents was refused, though
   its communications screen sends email, SMS and push to parents. "Changing records for you" competed
   with every screen a change is made on.
3. **The answer to a change request said "I can't do that for you."** Rule 20 is right that the assistant
   never acts; visitors read the sentence as "this app can't".
4. **The config was stale and nothing said so.** The app had moved its pages to new packages; the config
   still listed the old locale folders, which still existed. The build said "6 locale folders merged" while
   53% of the keys the pages use (1,264 of 2,387) resolved nowhere.

## Decision

1. **Controls are read, with who may use them** (`packages/generator/src/controls.ts`). From the page and the
   application's own components it renders, two levels down, never a design system's: a control is an
   *opener* (ADR-031), an *action* (its handler calls anything but state, navigation and event plumbing; a
   submit; an `AlertDialogTrigger`), or chrome (it moves, switches a view, closes, or is a lone dialog word —
   `isChrome`). Labels are the page's text or a translation key, never a computed string.
2. **Rule 17 for controls.** Every condition above a control is read against the permissions module discovery
   already reads for routes, and any pack's own (`RoleScope.listAt`, through imports and re-exports): role
   lists (`hasCapability(roles, ...CAPABILITY_ROLES.x)`), role literals (`hasRole('ADMIN')`, `role ===
   'TEACHER'`), `&&`, `||`, `!`, ternaries, early returns, local constants and helpers. A condition not about
   who narrows nothing. A condition about who that cannot be read **drops the control and reports it**
   (`review/CONTROLS.md`), as an unreadable route guard does. A control only some roles may use is absent
   from the others' registries (`actionRoles` / `opensRoles` stay in `master.json`, never shipped).
3. **`actions` is a registry field** (`core-capability`): validated like `opens`, matched in
   `capabilityDocument` (deduplicated against title and description, so a registry without it is unchanged)
   and as one of the screen's own phrasings in ADR-032's score, listed to stage 3 (ADR-043), and named —
   never pressed — when a visitor asks to do one. A control's label is never part of what the screen "shows":
   it reaches a registry through `actions`/`opens` only, so only the roles that may use it read it.
4. **A generic limit is declared only where nothing contradicts it, per role.** "Changing records for you" is
   removed: Rule 20 holds that in code. "Acting outside this application" is not declared to a role with a
   control that sends or books; "Processing a payment" not to one with a control that pays. Only controls are
   evidence — a purpose that says "the office handles fees" is not a pay button — and two Portuguese verbs
   that also mean something else are read in context: "marcar como lida" is marking, "transferir o PDF" is
   downloading.
5. **A limit does not refuse on words the best screen shares** — Rule 19's reading, for a refusal. A request
   to delete a child's record was refused as *fees*, by 0.004, on "meu filho". No number is involved.
6. **A change request is answered with where, and with what:** "Isso faz-se em Aulas · Visão geral. Use
   «Cancelar aula de apoio» lá. Eu não altero nada por si." (`controlFor`: by action concept, then shared
   words, then exact words, model-free; silent rather than close). A question about how much, when, which or
   whether ("quanto tenho de pagar este mês?") is not a change request.
7. **The build says when the pages' words are missing.** If a fifth or more of the translation keys the pages
   use (over twenty) resolve in no folder listed, the build warns and names the namespaces.

## Measured

A real app, 67 in-scope indirect questions and 6 out of scope, written before the change (not committed:
they describe that app). Same machine, same model, uncalibrated, as a first integration runs it:

| qwen3:14b stage 3 | Before | After (ADR-042 + ADR-043) |
|---|---|---|
| Right screen (moved, or offered for a change) | 45/67 (67%) | **58/67 (87%)** |
| Asked, with the right screen first | 1 | 4 |
| Moved to a wrong screen | 16 | **3** |
| Wrongly refused ("the app can't") | 5 | **0** |
| Out of scope refused | 6/6 | 6/6 |

Model-free on the same set: wrongly refused 1 → 0, asked with the right screen first 36 → 39 of 67, wrong
moves 0 → 0. **Out of scope refused model-free fell 2 → 1 of 6:** "transfere o dinheiro para o fornecedor"
is now asked rather than refused, because a "Transferir JSON" control (a data download) shares the stem and
narrows the payments limit's lead below the uncalibrated margin. Not a wrong move, and refused with the
model. On that app's largest role, screens with something to do went from 22 (openers only) to 37 of 68.

Reference fixture, model-free, recalibrated by the sweep (Rule 14; it kept every threshold): three turns
added before the fixture's controls were (`sch-0070`–`0072`) — all three right, `sch-0070` offered and the
other two moved; right screen 40/55 → 44/58; out of scope refused 71.4% → 71.4%; wrong moves 0; wrongly
refused 1 → 1 (the same turn). **One refusal became a question:** `sch-0065` ("altera a nota do aluno para
positiva") is now asked, one tap, because "Changing records for you" is no longer declared (item 4);
nothing is changed either way (Rule 20). `sch-0024` is now refused rather than asked, and `sch-0037` is
offered its screen.

Two things a fixture caught while this was built, and that the design now holds:

- **A control's name leaked through the page's copy.** "Justificar falta", for parents only, came out in
  another role's "This screen shows: Home, Excuse an absence". A control reaches a registry only through
  `actions`/`opens`, per role; its label is removed from what the screen "shows" (`generate-controls.test.ts`).
- **A control's name is a phrasing of its screen** (ADR-032's nearest-phrasing score), not two words lost in
  a whole document: model-free, "o meu filho vai faltar, quero justificar a falta" ranked another of the
  child's screens first on "o meu filho" until it was.

## Why not the alternatives

- **Drop every guarded control** (the first version). On a real app almost every control that changes data is
  role-gated, so its largest role kept openers on 22 screens and real actions on almost none. A registry
  that knows nothing a role can do is the failure this ADR exists for.
- **List guarded controls for everyone.** A parent would be told to press "Aprovar inscrição". Rule 17.
- **Keep the generic limits and let calibration sort it out.** A first integration is uncalibrated, as every
  new adopter is; a false refusal there is the first thing a visitor sees.
- **Ask a model at build time what each screen does.** Optional already (`--model`), and not used by the kit
  (ADR-021). The controls are written in the app; reading them is cheaper, reproducible and auditable.

## The reversibility test

A generator module, a registry field, a catalogue change, a stage-2 rule, a presenter branch. Reversible,
but it changes what every adopter's registry contains and what visitors read, so an ADR is owed.

## Consequences

- Registries are larger: up to 20 action names per screen, in every language the app ships.
- **What the scan cannot read, the assistant does not know.** A permission flag from a hook
  (`const { canDelete } = usePermissions()`) drops its control; `review/CONTROLS.md` lists every one.
- Controls in Vue, Svelte and Angular templates are read as text (2026-10-16), through the components a Vue
  or Svelte page imports; no role is evaluated there, so a control under any condition about who is left out
  and reported. Angular components rendered by selector are not followed.
- Whose an item is counts as "about who" (`article.author.username === user?.username`): a registry cannot
  say "its author only", so such a control is left out and reported.
- The concept table is still closed and bilingual (ADR-015); the two context rules (marcar, transferir) are
  Portuguese only.
- One fixture refusal became a question (above).

## Compliance

- `controls.test.ts` (generator): actions, openers, chrome, depth, roles from lists, literals, negation, a pack
  module, guarded and unreadable conditions, no permissions module.
- `emit.test.ts`: a control only some roles may use is absent from the others' registries, no role annotation
  is shipped, and `CONTROLS.md` reports it.
- `boundaries.test.ts`: no "changing records" limit; no limit the app's controls contradict.
- `acting-limit.test.ts`: a limit does not refuse on words the best screen shares.
- `action.test.ts`, `no-wrong-moves.test.ts`: `controlFor`, marking and downloading, questions are not changes.
- `present.test.ts`: "That's done in X", with the control, never "I can't do that for you".
- `untranslated.test.ts`, `labels-locales.test.ts`: the stale-config warning.
- `evaluate-golden.mjs --check` (CI): `sch-0070`–`0072`, and zero wrong moves.
