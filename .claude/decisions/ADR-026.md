# ADR-026 — The assistant is a chat panel beside the app, asks one question instead of offering a menu, and merges the app's own assistant in rather than competing with it

- **Status:** Accepted — by the owner, in session, 2026-09-25. Proposed 2026-09-23: the owner asked for
  each part in session. Amended by [ADR-038](./ADR-038.md) (a turn can be handed over by rule)
- **Date:** 2026-09-23
- **Deciders:** Andre, Architect
- **Relates to:** [ADR-013](./ADR-013.md) — no parallel navigation for an app that has one;
  [ADR-019](./ADR-019.md) — model prose, validated, never in "Why?"; [ADR-023](./ADR-023.md) — the likely
  one; [ADR-024](./ADR-024.md) and its addendum — attachments stay on the device, off by default;
  [ADR-025](./ADR-025.md) — a limit about acting; the Pilot-A field report,
  `nextgen/FEEDBACK-2026-09-23.md` (not committed: it lives in the adopter's folder).

## Context

The first real integration (Pilot-A: React 18 + React Router 7, 130 screens, 10 roles, JWT in memory, pt-PT)
was reported on 2026-09-23 by the integrating agent, and the owner used it themselves. What they met:

| Observed | Where it came from |
|---|---|
| "It says it is opening, and nothing happens" — route unchanged at +5 s, changed at +11 s | `ask()` awaited the answer being read aloud before moving; read-aloud was **on by default**; an utterance that never fires `end` waits 15 s |
| A second navigation appeared (a ☰ button top-left, a drawer) | the full-screen assistant covered the page and offered the app's links again in its own drawer (ADR-013 kept them the app's links, but it was still a second nav) |
| "Pergunte para onde quer ir" written beside the orb | the hover/focus hint |
| The full screen took over the app to answer anything | the only open state was a full-screen modal |
| Two assistants: NextGen's orb and the app's own model-backed chat ("Ask about your club's data"), plus a guided-tour prompt, all in the bottom-right corner | nothing in the product knew another assistant could exist |
| "I cannot import documents, or reset" | attachments are off unless the page opts in (ADR-024 addendum); start-over appeared only once a conversation existed |
| Clarifying answers read as a list of screens to choose between | `present.ts` offered up to two places as a menu: "Two places could help with that:" / "You probably want X." with "Or maybe:" beneath |

The owner's requirements, in their words: adopt the app's own elements rather than adding a navigation;
merge an existing assistant or tutorial in, "never losing features, but not messing up the bigger
picture"; voice muted by default; a lighter, more translucent background; no menu button, close on the
right; no text beside the orb; a smaller chat by default, full screen only when the visitor expands it,
with every button still there; and "avoid at maximum the several-option navigation — the user has no clue
where things are".

## Decision

**1. We will open the assistant as a chat panel in the orb's corner, and make full screen something the
visitor chooses.** The panel (at most 420 × 660; a bottom sheet on a phone) is a non-modal dialog: the page
beside it stays in view, scrolls, and takes Tab. Its header holds expand, start over (always shown), read
aloud and close. Expanded, it covers the screen **except the app's own navigation** — a sidebar against the
left edge or a bar across the top, measured on each expansion (`navFrame`, `nav.ts`) — and only then is it
modal. Reaching for that navigation returns it to its corner. The home route opens the panel, not the full
screen.

**2. We will have no navigation of our own.** The menu button and drawer are removed; the `menu` spoken
command with them. The app's navigation is read only to offer a lost visitor places to begin.

**3. We will move at once and speak only when asked.** A destination is navigated to as soon as the answer
is shown, never after it is read aloud or after an animation; the panel stays open beside the new page (in
its corner if it was expanded), and gives way only to a guide. Read-aloud defaults **off**; a hands-free
conversation is spoken regardless.

**4. We will answer an unsettled turn with one question, never a menu.** When one place leads (ADR-023's
bar, unchanged) or is the only match: "Are you looking for X?" with that one place, and "yes" takes it.
When two are close: "Do you mean X, or Y?" with those two. No third, no "Or maybe:". This is presentation
only: the router's thresholds and outcomes are untouched (Rule 14).

**5. We will merge an app's own assistant in as a `companion`, with this assistant as the one entry
point.** All optional:
- `ask({ text, files, route })` answers what this assistant cannot place: a turn where no candidate reaches
  the likely-one floor (every score below 0.15, ADR-023's own bar — refined from "all score 0" by ADR-027,
  after a content question scored 0.05 on two unrelated screens), an out-of-scope turn with no capability,
  and a declared limit about a **subject** with no `helpAt`
  (e.g. the generator's *questions about anything else*). **Never** a limit about acting (ADR-025's
  `refuses: 'action'`) and never one that already points somewhere. Its reply is shown as its own message,
  as plain text, under "Answered by <name>", with **no "Why?"** — this router did not decide it (Rule 9).
  Declining (`null`), empty, or throwing leaves the turn to this assistant, unchanged.
- Attached files go to `ask` with the words, instead of the `nextgen:attach` event.
- `open()` puts a button in the panel's header that opens theirs whole.
- `tour(route)` is started by the `tour` command ("tutorial", "visita guiada", "show me around"); without
  one, the page's declared steps; without those, the words are a question.
- `hide: [selectors]` hides their launchers while this one runs (a constructed sheet on the document,
  removed by `destroy()`); `data-nextgen-hide` gives the script tag the same.

**6. We will turn attachments on by default when a companion can receive them** (`companion.ask`), which
amends ADR-024's addendum: the reason for "off" was that a button that goes nowhere is a dead end, and with
a companion it goes somewhere. Without one, the default is still off.

## Alternatives considered

- **Do nothing.** Keeps every tested behaviour and every screen the owner rejected. The field report ranks
  the speech-gated move as the first thing that made the product look broken; the owner, independently,
  rejected the second nav and the full-screen default.
- **Keep full screen as the default and add a smaller mode behind a setting.** One setting per adopter,
  and the default is what every first visitor meets. The owner asked for the reverse.
- **Full screen covering the app's navigation, with our drawer removed.** Simpler — no measuring — but
  then the expanded view has no navigation at all, which is worse than the drawer it replaced.
- **Keep the menu of two with the leader first.** ADR-023's measurement stands (the right screen is among
  the first two 21 times in 21), and one question keeps the second place when it is close. What changes is
  the framing: a question can be answered by voice and in the visitor's words ("the injury one"), which
  the conversation memory already reads; a list asks them to rank names they have never seen.
- **Navigate on a clear lead below the router's threshold.** Fewest steps, but it moves on a guess the
  calibrated router declined to act on; a wrong move costs a page, a wrong question one "no".
- **Side by side: our orb plus a button that opens theirs.** Two entry points, which is the problem the
  owner and the report (F8) describe.
- **Route every question to the companion first.** Its model would answer navigation it cannot perform
  and would skip the validated registry path (ADR-019). Delegating only what this assistant cannot place
  keeps navigation where it is checked.

## Consequences

- **An answer this product did not write, and cannot check, now appears in its conversation.** The
  companion is the adopter's own assistant, under the adopter's own guardrails; this product labels it and
  shows no "Why?" for it, but a visitor may still hold the orb responsible for what it says. It also
  widens what reaches a model: questions and files that used to stay in the browser go to the adopter's
  function. That is the adopter's code and endpoint, not ours, and it is off unless they wire it — but
  Rule 8's "nothing leaves the device" is no longer true of a companion-enabled page, and the adopter must
  say so in their own privacy terms.
- **A subject limit can be answered by the companion.** "What's the weather tomorrow" was refused; with a
  companion it is whatever their assistant says. A declared limit on acting is still refused.
- **Attachments can now leave the device by default** — to the companion — which ADR-024 existed to avoid.
  Only when `companion.ask` is supplied.
- **One question can still be wrong in the same way a menu was.** It names at most two places, so a turn
  whose right screen ranked third is now "no" plus a rephrase instead of a third option that was never
  shown anyway (OFFERED was already 2).
- **The panel covers part of the page it leaves "in view".** 420 px of a 1440 px screen; after a move the
  new page is visible beside it, not under it. A visitor who wants the whole page closes it.
- **The expanded frame is a heuristic.** A left sidebar or a top bar is found by position and shape;
  a right-hand sidebar, a navigation inside a scrolling container, or a sidebar that is a `div` with no
  `nav`/`aside` inside is covered. The fallback is the old full screen, not a broken one.
- **Moving at once means the answer is not heard before the move** when read-aloud is on. It keeps being
  read on the new page, in the panel that stayed open.
- **Every adopter who integrated before this sees a different product on rebuild.** No option restores the
  full-screen default or the menu.
- **Size.** The script tag is 56.5 KB gzipped after this, from 54.9 KB (+1.6 KB), under the 60 KB test
  budget with 3.5 KB left.

## Compliance

- **Enforced by tests** (`packages/embed`): `assistant.test.ts` — the panel is non-modal and compact by
  default; expanded is modal, traps Tab, locks scroll and leaves a measured sidebar uncovered; no menu or
  drawer exists; nothing is written beside the orb; the move happens before a voice finishes; read-aloud is
  off until turned on; the companion is asked only for unplaced turns and subject limits, never an acting
  limit, gets files instead of the page, is attributed with no "Why?", and its failure falls back; its
  launchers are hidden and restored; `nav.test.ts` — `navFrame`; `present.test.ts` — one question, one or
  two places; `commands.test.ts` — `tour`, no `menu`; `script-tag.test.ts` — `nextgen:error`,
  `data-nextgen-inset`, `data-nextgen-hide`.
- **Enforced by gate:** `check-accessibility.mjs` — non-modal panel, modal when expanded, the toggle's
  name, Tab kept in when expanded, no navigation of its own.
- **By convention only:** that an adopter's `companion.ask` does not answer with navigation it cannot
  perform, and that an adopter discloses that questions and files reach their assistant. Nothing here can
  check either.
