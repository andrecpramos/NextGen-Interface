# ADR-032 — A screen is scored against all of it and against its nearest phrasing, averaged

- **Status:** Accepted — by the owner, in session, 2026-09-25. Proposed 2026-09-23: the owner asked for it
  ("keep improving our model standard reasoning, I want it to be able to comprehend the user, regarding any
  section present in the adopter web app. Make sure this isn't Pilot-A specific"). **Its measurement omitted
  wrong moves:** with this scoring the family fixture executed 4 in-scope turns on a wrong screen (1
  before) and 1 out-of-scope request (0 before). [ADR-041](./ADR-041.md) makes that a gated failure
- **Date:** 2026-09-23
- **Deciders:** Andre, Architect
- **Relates to:** Rule 14 (thresholds are calibrated, never hand-set); [ADR-009](./ADR-009.md) (limits are
  retrieval, not a threshold); [ADR-028](./ADR-028.md) (sparse vectors, scale); [ADR-011](./ADR-011.md) (the
  golden gate).

## Context

Without a model, understanding is stage 2: a question is embedded (the hashing TF-IDF encoder, or nomic in
the lab) and compared with one vector per capability, built from its whole document: title, description,
triggers and every example blended together.

The family fixture's misses on 2026-09-23 were not what they looked like. Of 16 in-scope questions not
settled correctly, **8 had the right screen ranked first** but below the calibrated bar ("tenho avisos por
ler", "estou apto para jogar?"). **6 had it second**, behind a near neighbour: notification *settings*
against the notification *inbox*, or two "overview" screens. By ranking alone the right screen came first
for 46 of 55. The shortfall was **confidence and separation**, not vocabulary. The word bridge added
nothing to any of them, because every word was already in the registry.

The cause is the blend. A short, specific question ("notificações por ler") has to match the average of
everything a screen says. The example that says almost exactly that is diluted by the rest of the
document, and two screens about the same subject end up with near-identical averages.

This is a property of the scoring, not of Pilot-A. It applies to every generated registry.

## Decision

We will score each capability as the **mean of two cosines**:

- the question against the capability's **whole document**, as before;
- the question against the **nearest of its own phrasings**: its title and each example, embedded one by one.

Limits (`unsupported`) are scored by the same rule. There is one rule, and it was measured both ways (below).

The phrasing vectors are built once per registry and embedder, on first use, with the same fitted
embedder that embeds the question, and they are stored sparse (`embedSparse`). The shipped index format
does not change: the widget builds them on its first question.

Every threshold is re-derived by its sweep, and none is set by hand: `THRESHOLD_PROFILES` (reference
registry, both encoders, transcribed from `eval/calibrate.js --defer-oos`) and the family fixture's
`thresholds.hashing.json` (`calibrate-registries.mjs`).

## Alternatives considered

**Do nothing.** 46 of 55 were already ranked first, and a "Looking for X?" question costs a tap. But the
owner asked for understanding, not better questions, and 7 more settled correctly with no wrong move is
exactly that.

**Nearest phrasing only (k-NN over examples).** It ranked 47 of 55 first, but it over-trusts one example:
a single generic example pulls unrelated questions. The average ranked 49, and settled more.

**The maximum of the two.** It accepted slightly more on the reference registry (24.6%) but caught the
fewest out-of-scope questions (6/13) of any variant. Rejected: refusal is where a wrong call costs most.

**Averaged screens, limits by document.** Its refusal on the reference registry (8/13) is one better and
on the family fixture (64.3%) one worse. It needs two rules for one idea. It is recorded above in case a
later golden set tips the balance.

**More vocabulary (bridge concepts, synonyms).** The probe showed the bridge added nothing to any miss:
every word was already known. More words would not have moved these questions.

**Ship phrasing vectors in the index.** That saves the first-question build (150 ms at 2,000 screens) at
the cost of an index several times larger for every adopter. Not worth it at these numbers.

## Consequences

- **Every adopter's scores change, so every calibration is stale.** An adopter's `calibration.json` was
  swept under the old score. Until they re-run `calibrate`, the kit's shipped profile (recalibrated here)
  is the better bet. `doctor` already reports an old calibration; the adapter guide tells them to re-run it.
- **Out-of-scope refusal on the reference registry fell by two** (9/13 → 7/13). Those questions are now
  *asked about* rather than refused. None is executed wrongly, but a refusal is the clearer answer, and it
  was measured on 13 questions.
- **Examples matter more.** One misleading example on a screen now pulls questions toward it more than
  before. The generator's examples and the adopter's `askedAs` carry more weight, for better and worse.
- **The first question pays for the phrasings:** 15 ms at 100 screens, 150 ms at 2,000.
- **Measured on two golden sets**, one generated app (69 turns) and one hand registry (81). A third app
  may disagree. The gate holds the family decisions; nothing holds a real adopter's.
