# ADR-028 — Built for complex apps: a sparse index, item screens reached through their list, screens the adopter declares, and a numbered path to improve it

- **Status:** Accepted — by the owner, in session, 2026-09-25. Proposed 2026-09-23: the owner asked for it ("improve this so it can perform
  better in different applications with high complexity, the best we can"; "make it clear for the adopter
  that he can do so, and it will help by doing steps 1/2/3")
- **Date:** 2026-09-23
- **Deciders:** Andre, Architect
- **Relates to:** [ADR-024](./ADR-024.md) (WP-24, the shipped index); [ADR-026](./ADR-026.md);
  [ADR-027](./ADR-027.md) — `indexFor`, several roles per user, the dial, lessons; Rule 10 (three examples),
  Rule 14 (thresholds are calibrated), Rule 17 (a role's registry is its entitlement).

## Context

Four things stood between the product and an application of real complexity.

**1. Size.** The hashing encoder's vectors are as wide as the registry's whole vocabulary, and were
stored and shipped dense — zeros included. Measured on synthetic registries, 2026-09-23 (words drawn
from a 6,000-word vocabulary, realistic document lengths):

| Screens | Index (dense) | Build the index | Per question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 3.2 MB | 0.19 s | 1.6 ms |
| 500 | 29 MB | 2.0 s | 15 ms |
| 2,000 | 150 MB | 10.5 s | 72 ms |

An app of a few hundred screens could not be loaded by a browser. Pilot-A (130 screens, ≤ 68 per role) was
under the cliff; the next adopter may not be.

**2. Item screens.** Most screens of a complex app are about one item — `/players/:id/stats`,
`/invoices/:id`. From a page that names no item, a decided turn answered "open that item, then ask again",
and a clarifying turn silently dropped the screen the visitor most likely meant.

**3. Repeated names.** "Settings", "Overview", "Reports" appear in several areas. Offered side by side,
"Settings, or Settings?" cannot be answered.

**4. Screens the scanner cannot read.** Routes decided on the server, micro-frontends and frameworks the
scanner does not know leave screens out of the registry, and there was no way to put them back: a
`knowledge.json` entry for such a route was reported as an orphan.

And one about the adopter: the levers to improve the assistant existed (knowledge, calibration,
teaching, the dial, search, their own assistant, a model), but nothing told them, in order, what to do
next and what it would give them.

## Decision

1. **We will store and ship hashing vectors sparse** (`core-intent/src/sparse.ts`): each vector as its
   non-zero `idx`/`val`, in memory and in `index.<ROLE>.json` (`format: 'sparse'`, `dimension`,
   entries of `terms`/`weights`). `cosine` takes dense or sparse on either side; the encoder gains
   `embedSparse`. The weights and their rounding are unchanged, and zeros add nothing to a dot product,
   so every score — and every decision — is identical. A dense index (every one built before this) is
   still read. A real embedding model's vectors stay dense.
2. **We will reach an item screen through its list.** `listFor` finds the screen an item screen is chosen
   from — the longest route prefix before its first item parameter (a locale is not one). A decided turn
   for an item screen the page does not name offers that list ("open Players, choose the one you want");
   a clarifying turn offers it in the item screen's place, saying what to do there.
3. **We will tell two screens with one name apart by their area** — the title of the screen above
   (`/finance/settings` under `/finance`), else the route's first segment: "Settings · Finance".
4. **We will let the adopter declare a screen the scanner cannot read**: a `knowledge.json` `screens` entry
   keyed by a route that matches nothing found, with a `title`, becomes a screen (`declaredScreens`), with
   Rule 10's examples in the app's language and everything else `knowledge.json` gives a found screen. In
   an app with more than one role it must name its `roles`, or it is left out and `KNOWLEDGE-GAPS.md` says
   why: nothing is guessed into an entitlement.
5. **We will give the adopter a numbered path** — six steps, in order of value for effort (describe the
   screens; calibrate; wire it in; teach it from real use; choose how bold it is and where the rest goes;
   optionally a model) — printed by `doctor` with where the app stands on each (✓ done, → next, ? only the
   adopter can tell), written by `build` to `review/IMPROVE.md`, and described in AGENTS.md, which tells the
   installing agent to say so up front. The optional model is never marked "next".

## Measured

After item 1, same synthetic registries:

| Screens | Index | Build the index | Per question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 0.4 MB | 0.03 s | 0.7 ms |
| 500 | 1.8 MB | 0.11 s | 1.7 ms |
| 2,000 | **7.3 MB** | **0.38 s** | **2.8 ms** |

On the reference fixture the three committed indexes shrank by 38% (67 → 41 KB for COACH), and every gate
that compares decisions — generated-golden, derived-artefacts' browser parity, the scorecard — reports no
decision changed. Items 2–4 change presentation and the registry only where an adopter uses them; the
fixture's decisions are unchanged.

## Alternatives considered

- **Do nothing.** Fine for Pilot-A's size; an app of 500 screens downloads 29 MB before its first question.
- **Hash features into a fixed width** (the "hashing trick"). Bounded size, but collisions change scores,
  every threshold would need recalibrating (lessons L1), and the "a word no screen uses contributes nothing"
  property the out-of-scope behaviour leans on would blur.
- **Compute the index in the browser and ship none.** `indexFor` makes it possible, and a multi-role user
  already does; for one role, the shipped file saves the first question a few hundred milliseconds on a
  large app, and it is now small.
- **Offer the item screen with a generic "open an item first".** What there was. It names nothing the
  visitor can act on.
- **Declare unreadable screens in `nextgen.config.json`.** A second place to describe a screen; the
  adopter already describes screens in `knowledge.json`, and the declared one is enriched the same way.
- **Default a declared screen to every role.** Convenient, and a way to put a screen in the registry of a
  role that cannot open it — the disclosure Rule 17 exists to prevent.
- **A settings page for the steps instead of text.** The adopter's tool is a CLI and their agent reads
  AGENTS.md; the steps go where they already look.

## Consequences

- **The index file format changed.** Anything that read `entries[i].vector` directly — Pilot-A's integrator
  built a step that reproduces indexes from the minified runtime — breaks on a sparse file. Several roles
  are now merged in the browser (ADR-027), which removes the reason for that step.
- **7 MB for 2,000 screens is still a download** (smaller compressed, and served per role). A very large
  app should serve its bundle compressed and cached.
- **The query is still embedded dense** — one array as wide as the vocabulary per question, 37,000 numbers
  at 2,000 screens: cheap, but not free.
- **A list offered for an item screen can be wrong** when the route prefix is not really its list (a
  `/players/:id` under `/players` that is a dashboard, not a list). The wording says what to do there, not
  that it is the list.
- **A declared screen is only as right as its declaration**: its route, title and roles are served as the
  adopter wrote them. A route that does not exist is a dead link to the visitor.
- **The steps can go stale** — "calibrated" says nothing about how long ago, and "taught" only that
  `teach` ran once.

## Compliance

- **Enforced by tests:** `sparse.test.ts` (sparse equals the non-zeros of dense; dense×sparse and
  sparse×sparse score exactly as dense×dense; a sparse and a dense index rank alike; kilobytes per screen,
  not megabytes; malformed sparse indexes refused; dense still read), `registry-browser.test.ts` (`indexFor`
  reproduces every committed index), `present.test.ts` (item screens through their list; areas),
  `knowledge.test.ts` (declared screens: roles required in a multi-role app, valid ids, three examples,
  merged like found screens, not orphans), `ladder.test.ts`.
- **Enforced by gates:** generated-golden, derived-artefacts (indexes rebuilt sparse, browser parity on every
  golden turn), scorecard, adapter-stale.
- **By convention only:** that a declared screen's route, title and roles are true.
