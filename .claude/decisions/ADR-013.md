# ADR-013 — The product is a drop-in AI navigation layer for an existing app, not a personal assistant

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-09-09
- **Deciders:** Andre, Architect
- **Supersedes:** —
- **Amends:** [ADR-005](./ADR-005.md) (scope, not conclusion — see below), [ADR-008](./ADR-008.md)
  (re-scoped from "the device" to "the browser" — see below)

## Context

**This is the third pivot, and the first one with an ADR.** Two landed before it with
their reasoning only in commit messages:

1. **WP-01–WP-09** built a personal finance tracker: local-first, one user, one device,
   `domain-finance`'s 20 capabilities.
2. **WP-14** (2026-08-31) replaced the registered domain with `domain-modules` — a
   "remember this" notes app — with, in its own retrospective's words, **"no ADR or
   PLAN entry ahead of this work."** [.claude/docs/history.md](../docs/history.md) §WP-14 records
   this as the single largest process gap that pivot created.
3. **2026-09-08/09**, the owner repositioned the product itself, stated directly rather
   than discovered in code: not a personal assistant we design and ship end to end, but
   a **drop-in AI navigation layer for a web app that already exists.** An adopter points
   the tool at their app's source; it generates a capability registry; their users get an
   assistant that navigates, guides and explains against that registry. `fut-app` — 146
   pages, 30 features, role-gated, an existing `CommandPalette` to measure against — is
   the pilot (PLAN.md §3).

Two product requirements were stated alongside this, binding: (1) the adopter installs
this into their own codebase — a package they add, never a service we run, and the
installer touches nothing of theirs (**Rule 16**); (2) it learns their app and becomes an
add-on UI layer covering navigate/read/explain **and nothing else** — enforced by declared
boundaries without a model call ([ADR-009](./ADR-009.md)), not a prompt instruction.

This ADR exists to do three things the WP-14 pivot did not: record what changed and why,
say what it means for the two ADRs closest to it, and answer the open decision (**D-1**,
PLAN.md §13) that repositioning forced — do the two reference applications survive?

## Decision

**The product is: a build-time generator that reads an existing app's source and role
and emits a role-scoped capability registry (Rule 17), plus a browser runtime, shipped
with the adopter's build, that resolves most turns with zero network calls.**

`domain-finance` (20 capabilities, 34 tests) and `domain-modules` (7 capabilities, 39
tests) are **reclassified, not removed**. Neither is the product. Both become **reference
applications** — the generator's ground truth. **D-1 = keep both.**

### Why keep both (D-1)

The only honest quality measure a generator has, before a second, external pilot exists,
is **precision and recall against a registry a careful developer would have written by
hand** (WP-25's acceptance criterion). Two independently-shaped, hand-built registries —
one a 20-capability financial app, one a 7-capability notes app, built for different
reasons at different times — are a harder and more honest target than one. A
purpose-built fixture app, written *to* score the generator, would be built to pass;
these two were not.

**Cost of choosing wrong.** Keeping both means [.claude/docs/history.md](../docs/history.md) and
future docs work (WP-11) must explain, to a reader who has not seen this ADR, that
neither app is the product — a confusion this file exists partly to prevent. Dropping one
would have cost the generator a comparison point that cannot be recovered cheaply once
gone.

### The reversibility test

Reversible, deliberately. Nothing about this decision deletes code, changes a schema, or
forecloses an option: both reference apps keep passing their own tests unchanged: the
generator (WP-25, not yet built) is what will actually depend on them, and it reads their
registries, not their runtime behaviour. If a future session finds one reference app is
not earning its keep, retiring it is a one-package deletion, not a migration.

## Alternatives considered

**D-1(b) — keep one, drop the other.** Rejected: halves the generator's only ground truth
before it exists, to save documentation effort that WP-11 has to spend either way.

**D-1(c) — replace both with a purpose-built fixture app.** Rejected: a fixture written to
exercise the generator is written *with the generator's shape in mind*, which is precisely
what makes it a weak test of that generator. `domain-finance` and `domain-modules` were
each built for reasons unrelated to scoring anything, which is what makes them useful now.

**Do nothing — keep building the personal assistant, absorb the repositioning as a future
concern.** Rejected by the owner directly, not by this document: the commercial reasoning
is in [business-case.md](../docs/business-case.md), and PLAN.md §1.2 states the thesis this
answers — an assistant that resolves most turns with no model call can sit in front of
every visitor of every adopter; one that needs a frontier-model call per turn cannot. That
argument does not exist for a personal, single-user app.

## What this means for ADR-005

[ADR-005](./ADR-005.md) decided: *"the assistant is primary; a complete conventional
navigation structure exists underneath it."* That instruction was written for an app we
build end to end, and **it still governs exactly that** — `apps/web`'s own surface over
the reference applications keeps its nav drawer, its route coverage, its four rationales
(accessibility, discoverability, failure degradation, speed).

**It does not extend to an installed adopter deployment (Ladder C2/C3), and does not need
to.** `fut-app` already has 146 routable pages, role-gated navigation, and a
`CommandPalette`. Rule 16 forbids the installer from touching any of it. Rule 7's
guarantee — every screen the assistant can reach stays reachable without it — is
satisfied **by construction**, because we are additive-only: the adopter's own
navigation, which already covered 100% of their routes before we existed, keeps doing so
after. We do not build a second, parallel navigation structure for an app that already
has one. No conflict; a narrowed scope, stated once rather than rediscovered per adopter.

## What this means for ADR-008

[ADR-008](./ADR-008.md) decided: the device is the source of truth for the user's data,
nothing on the critical path may require the network, and inference stays local by
default. Re-read for this product (already stated as **Rule 8** in PLAN.md §2, confirmed
here rather than left as an inline aside):

- **"The device is the source of truth"** was about *our own app's* financial data in
  local SQLite. The general product holds none of an adopter's user data at all — the
  registry and its embedding index are static artefacts generated at the adopter's build
  time (Ladder B1) and shipped with their bundle. There is no local database to be
  authoritative over; the rule does not carry forward because its subject no longer
  exists in the new product. It still applies, unchanged, to the reference apps.
- **"Nothing on the critical path may require the network"** generalises cleanly and
  becomes *more* central, not less: stages 1–2 run in the visitor's browser against the
  precomputed bundle, with zero network calls — the same guarantee ADR-008 wrote for a
  personal device, now true for anyone's browser tab. This is the entire cost argument in
  PLAN.md §1.2.
- **"Inference stays local by default"** becomes: stage-3 escalation is an endpoint the
  *adopter* configures and pays for; no key ever reaches the browser (WP-26's acceptance
  criterion already says this). We do not run inference on the adopter's behalf.

**`services/api`'s role narrows to near-nothing under rungs A1/B1/C1.** Its original
purposes — sync for a personal app's local data, model-manifest hosting for a downloaded
router model — do not exist in this product: there is no personal data to sync, and the
browser's encoder ships as a static bundle rather than something fetched at runtime.
**Decision: keep the package, empty, reserved for Ladder B2's hosted trace store** (the
one purpose on ADR-008's original list that still has a plausible future home) **and use
it for nothing else without a new ADR** — the same discipline ADR-008 itself asked for.
This answers the question [.claude/docs/history.md](../docs/history.md) §4's dead-weight
register left open: `services/api` is not dead, and it is not currently used; both are
true, and the second is deliberate.

**No conflict found with either ADR that would trigger this work package's stop
condition.** Both needed rescoping, not reversal — the four sessions of engine work
neither ADR's conclusions depend on (the cascade, the policy table, `TurnTrace`, the
registry format) are unaffected.

## Consequences

**Good**

- Two independently-built, unrelated-in-origin registries give WP-25 real precision/recall
  ground truth instead of a fixture built to pass its own test.
- Neither ADR-005 nor ADR-008 needed to be reversed — the rescoping is a paragraph each,
  not a rewrite, and the engine built against their original conclusions stays valid.
- `services/api`'s status stops being an open question revisited every audit — it has a
  stated, narrow, deliberate reason to exist unused.

**Bad, or at least a cost**

- WP-11 (documentation) must now explain, in every doc a newcomer might land on first,
  that neither reference app is the product — a confusion this ADR exists to prevent but
  cannot itself fix everywhere.
- **`domain-finance` is orphaned** (PLAN.md §4, found 2026-09-09): no workspace package
  depends on it, and `apps/cli` cannot register it, so the "swap the registered
  application and prove the engine is domain-agnostic" test that D-1's own justification
  rests on **is not currently executable.** Keeping both reference apps only pays for
  itself once this is fixed. Tracked as required, not optional, follow-up work — landing
  alongside WP-16, whose own acceptance criterion ("swapping the registered application
  changes zero lines in any `core-*` package") needs exactly this wiring to be testable.
- `services/api` remains in the tree, tested by nothing, doing nothing, for a rung (B2)
  the plan may never climb. Deliberate per above, but a real, ongoing cost: it is exactly
  the shape of surface ADR-008 warned attracts scope through drift, and staying inert
  requires someone to keep saying no, not just this document having said so once.

## Compliance

Convention, not a gate. PLAN.md §1 and §3 must not contradict this ADR — true as of this
session's §5/§3 updates — and `check-doc-links.mjs` verifies the links here resolve, but
nothing currently checks narrative agreement between PLAN.md and an ADR's content. Noted
as a gap for [.claude/docs/critical-infrastructure.md](../docs/critical-infrastructure.md) rather
than invented a check for here.
