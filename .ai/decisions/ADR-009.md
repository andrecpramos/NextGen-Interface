# ADR-009 — Out-of-scope is declared in the registry, not judged by the model

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-08-29
- **Deciders:** Architect, AI Engineer
- **Supersedes:** the out-of-scope mechanism in [ADR-003](ADR-003.md) (stage-2 `τ_floor`,
  then the stage-3 `__out_of_scope__` sentinel). The sentinel stays, but it is no longer
  the mechanism — it is now the second of two.
- **Evidence:** [FINDINGS.md](../../packages/core-intent/eval/FINDINGS.md) Findings 2, 7, 11.

## Context

WP-01 left the architecture with no working way to tell that a request is outside the
application at all.

The history is three attempts, each refuted by measurement:

1. **ADR-003 as written:** reject at stage 2 when no candidate clears `τ_floor`.
   Calibration found no operating point on *either* encoder (Finding 2). `"more"` scores
   0.154 and `"what time is it"` scores 0.127; no threshold sits between them.
2. **Finding 2's fix:** move the judgement to stage 3 as an `__out_of_scope__` value in
   the constrained enum. This worked — 85–90% on `llama3.1:8b`.
3. **WP-01:** it does not survive a smaller model. `qwen2.5:1.5b` catches 30–40%.
   `gemma3:1b` catches **0%** — it never emits the sentinel once in 140 constrained
   calls, and answers *"what is the capital of France"* with `settings.currency` at 0.95
   confidence (Finding 7).

So: stage 2 could not do it, and stage 3 could not do it on the hardware we ship to.

### Why "fit a better classifier" is not the answer

Before choosing, the obvious next idea was measured: Finding 2 only ever tested a
*single scalar cut on the top score*, which is a much weaker test than "the geometry
cannot separate these". Five features were extracted for all 200 golden-set utterances
(top score, margin, mean, softmax entropy, spread) and swept.

The best rule found, at a budget of ≤2% of routable turns lost: **20% out-of-scope
recall**. Barely better than one feature.

The distributions show why, and the reason is not noise:

| | top score |
|---|---|
| `"what's my credit score"` (out of scope) | 0.637 |
| `"cancel my netflix subscription for me"` (out of scope) | 0.593 |
| `"transfer 500 to my brother"` (out of scope) | 0.500 |
| `"no not that one"` (in domain, multi-turn) | 0.363 |
| `"what about transport"` (in domain, multi-turn) | 0.414 |

The two classes are genuinely interleaved, because **out-of-scope requests in a finance
app are mostly about finance**. "Pay my electricity bill" is not off-topic; it is
on-topic and unsupported. Meanwhile in-domain anaphora carries almost no semantic
content at all. No boundary in that space separates them, because the thing that
distinguishes them is not *what the sentence is about* — it is *what the application
does*.

And the application already knows what it does. [ADR-004](ADR-004.md) rule 3 states it
outright: "No capability performs an irreversible external effect. No payments, no
transfers, no sending." That boundary was written down in prose, in an ADR, where no
code could read it.

## Decision

**The registry declares the application's limits, as first-class entries.**

A new capability kind, `unsupported`, describes a class of requests the application
deliberately does not serve. It carries a description, triggers, examples and a
`refusal` message — and no route and no params, enforced by the validator.

```ts
defineBoundary({
  id: 'unsupported.move_money',
  title: 'Moving real money',
  description:
    'Making a payment, sending or transferring funds to another person, business, ' +
    'biller or account. Settling a bill. Any action that moves real money rather ' +
    'than recording that money moved.',
  examples: ['send some money to a friend', 'settle the gas bill', 'move funds into my savings account'],
  refusal:
    "I can record and analyse your transactions, but I can't move real money. " +
    'Nothing here is connected to a payment provider.',
});
```

Boundary entries are retrieved by **exactly the same machinery as every other
capability** — same encoder, same documents, same cosine. Out-of-scope detection stops
being a judgement and becomes routing.

Two rules govern them:

1. **`boundaryMargin`.** A boundary entry only wins if it beats the best *real*
   capability by a calibrated margin. A boundary describes a whole class, so it is broad
   by construction and attracts weak matches that belong elsewhere. Like every other
   threshold this is **an artefact of the encoder** — 0.105 for TF-IDF, 0.075 for
   `nomic-embed-text`, and carrying one onto the other costs real accuracy. Calibrated by
   `calibrate.js`, never hand-set (Finding 1, again).
2. **Stage 3 never sees them.** A refusal is not something the model gets to choose: it
   already has a sentinel for "none of these", and giving it a second way to refuse only
   gives it a way to refuse wrongly.

The calibration objective is deliberately asymmetric: **maximise out-of-scope recall
subject to refusing zero in-domain turns.** A missed refusal wastes a turn. A wrong
refusal tells the user the app cannot do something it demonstrably can, which is how
people stop trusting a product.

## Results

Measured on the 200-entry golden set, seven boundary entries, `nomic-embed-text`:

| Stage-3 model | Out-of-scope before | after | model's contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| `llama3.1:8b` | 85% | 85% | large — its sentinel already worked |
| `qwen2.5:1.5b` | 40% | **60%** | 20 points |
| `gemma3:1b` | **0%** | **40%** | **none — 40% is entirely model-free** |

**The mechanism helps in exact proportion to how badly the model fails.** It puts a
floor under the worst case and costs nothing in the best case, which is the property
that makes it safe to depend on. The 40% floor is identical on the model that refuses
nothing, because no model is involved in reaching it.

Nothing regressed:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Top-1, `direct` (`qwen2.5:1.5b`) | 97.5% | **100.0%** |
| Top-1, `paraphrase` | 75.0% | **76.7%** |
| Top-1 overall | 80.0% | 80.0% |
| Param exactness | 98.2% | 98.2% |
| Model-free resolution | 30.0% | **35.0%** |
| Adversarial safety violations | 0 | 0 |
| Turn latency p95 | 151ms | 168ms |

## Consequences

**Positive**

- Out-of-scope detection works on the model we ship, which was not previously true.
- A declared refusal is a *better answer* than a generic one. The user is told what the
  app does instead, in a message authored next to the thing being refused.
- The boundary is now a diff. Adding a capability that moves money means deleting the
  entry that forbids it — a conspicuous thing to have to do in a code review, and
  exactly the review [ADR-004](ADR-004.md) requires.
- It composes. Stage 2 catches what it can model-free; the stage-3 sentinel still runs
  for the rest. The two are independent, so the weaker the model, the more the first
  one carries.
- Boundary entries are ordinary registry entries, so they get validation, similarity
  auditing and codegen for free.

**Negative**

- **Recall depends on how well the boundary is written.** 40% model-free is a floor, not
  a solution — it is bounded by the seven classes declared and how well they were
  phrased. Missing classes are missed refusals.
- Another calibrated threshold per encoder, and one more thing that must be re-swept
  when the encoder or the registry changes.
- Boundary entries sit closer to real capabilities than real capabilities sit to each
  other, so they crowd the space. `boundaryAudit()` exists to make that visible, and the
  margin is what keeps it safe.
- The registry now contains entries that are not features, which needs explaining to
  anyone reading it for the first time.

**What this does not solve**

- **The remaining 40–60%.** Utterances like *"what is the capital of France"* and
  *"who won the football last night"* are caught by the 8B's sentinel and not by the
  boundary, because no declared class covers general knowledge well enough. Widening
  `unsupported.general` is the obvious lever and was deliberately not pulled here — the
  boundary was written from the domain's own limits, not from the test set, and tuning
  it against the golden set would measure nothing.
- **Non-English.** Boundary entries are English, like the rest of the registry, and
  Portuguese in-domain utterances drift toward them. The margin currently keeps that at
  zero false refusals, but the underlying problem — an English-only registry — is
  unchanged and still owed a decision.
- **The confidence gate** (Finding 8) is a separate problem and is not addressed here.

## Enforcement

- The validator rejects a boundary entry with a route, with params, or without a refusal
  message, and rejects a `refusal` on an executable capability.
- `generateRouteTable` excludes `unsupported` by construction, so a refusal can never
  become navigable.
- `TAU_ACT_BY_KIND` is keyed by `ExecutableKind`, so there is no confidence at which a
  declared limit could be allowed to run — indexing it with a possible `'unsupported'`
  does not compile.
- The dispatcher distinguishes a *declared* refusal from an *unclassified* one. They
  look the same to the user and are opposites to the developer: one is the product
  working, the other must fail the build.
