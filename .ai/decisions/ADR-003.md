# ADR-003 — Hybrid three-stage intent router, not LLM-only

- **Status:** **Accepted** — validated against 1B-class models on 2026-08-29 (WP-01).
  Three claims below are amended by that measurement; each is marked inline.
- **Date:** 2026-08-29 · **Validated:** 2026-08-29
- **Deciders:** Architect, AI Engineer
- **Supersedes:** —
- **Evidence:** [eval/FINDINGS.md](../../packages/core-intent/eval/FINDINGS.md),
  Part II. Scorecards in `packages/core-intent/eval/scorecards/`.

## Context

The blueprint's implied intent design is: send the utterance to a small local model,
receive JSON describing intent and parameters. This is the most common design for
AI-native navigation, and it is the most common reason such projects fail.

The constraints it has to satisfy simultaneously:

- p95 turn latency < 2s on a mid-range Android phone
- Fully offline
- A model small enough to ship (~700 MB int4 at 1B parameters)
- A registry that grows to 60+ capabilities
- Financial writes, where a wrong action is materially worse than no action

A 1B model asked to select from 60 capabilities and extract typed parameters from
free-form speech does not reliably satisfy these. Prompt length alone pushes latency;
accuracy on long option lists is poor; and every turn paying full inference cost blows
the latency and battery budgets.

## Decision

A **three-stage cascade**, cheapest first, with the model used only where it adds value.

```
Stage 1  DETERMINISTIC   <5ms     exact / alias / regex over registry triggers
Stage 2  EMBEDDING       <60ms    cosine match vs precomputed capability vectors,
                                  accept on score ≥ τ_high AND margin ≥ δ
Stage 3  LLM             <800ms   grammar-constrained selection among stage-2 top-k
                                  + slot filling. Confidence < τ_act → Clarify.
```

Supporting decisions:

- **Runtime:** llama.cpp. Portable across Android/iOS/desktop, mature quantisation,
  and — decisively — grammar support for constrained decoding. *(The `dart:ffi`
  binding named in the original text is superseded by [ADR-006](ADR-006.md); the
  runtime choice is unchanged, the binding is now `llama.rn` / Ollama behind the
  `core-inference` adapter interface.)*
- **Constrained decoding is mandatory.** Never parse free-form model output.
- **Stage 3 sees only the top-k candidates**, never the full registry.
- **The model never computes numbers and never emits SQL or raw routes.**
- **Clarify is a first-class outcome.** Refusing to guess is correct behaviour.

## Rationale

**Latency is met by not calling the model.** If 65%+ of turns resolve at stages 1–2,
the p95 budget is comfortable. If every turn hits inference, it is unachievable on the
target hardware. This single property drives the whole design.

**The model's job shrinks to something a 1B model can do.** "Pick one of these 5
labelled options and extract a date range" is tractable at 1B. "Recall the right one of
60 capabilities described in a 3000-token prompt" is not.

**Registry growth stops being a latency and accuracy tax.** Capability #61 grows a
vector index, not a prompt.

**Grammar constraints guarantee valid JSON — not correct JSON.** This is the point most
often missed. Constrained decoding makes parse failure *rare*, not impossible — measured
at 1 in ~140 calls on `llama3.1:8b` and 0 in ~280 on the two 1B-class models — and does
nothing for semantic errors. **The parse-failure fallback is mandatory, not defensive.**
*(Amended by Finding 6; the original text claimed parse failures were eliminated
entirely. Separately: 0 hallucinated capability ids in ~1000 constrained calls across
four models — the schema enum itself has held without exception.)* Confidence gating plus `Clarify` handles those, and the dispatcher
policy ([ADR-004](ADR-004.md)) contains the damage when they slip through.

**Injection defence falls out of the architecture.** Because stage 3 can only choose
among candidates derived from the *user's utterance*, malicious text stored in a
transaction memo cannot introduce a destructive capability into the candidate set.

## Consequences

**Positive**

- Latency, battery, and offline viability all improve together
- Accuracy improves — deterministic paths are exactly right, not probably right
- Graceful degradation: model missing or failed → stages 1–2 still route, worst case a
  `Clarify` with tappable options. The app never hangs and never guesses.
- Testable: each stage is measurable independently

**Negative**

- Three components to build and tune instead of one
- Four thresholds (τ_high, δ, τ_act, τ_floor) require calibration against a real corpus.
  **These are versioned artefacts, not constants** (Finding 1): τ_high and δ belong to
  the *encoder*, τ_act to the *model*, and `topK` to the *model* as well. Changing
  either component invalidates the corresponding thresholds. Guessing τ_high at a
  plausible-looking 0.62 once disabled stage 2 entirely and cost 72 points of accuracy.
- An embedding model (~20 MB) plus a precomputed index adds build complexity
- Registry descriptions must be written for semantic distinctness, which is a genuine
  ongoing authoring discipline

## Validation — measured, 2026-08-29 (WP-01)

The gate this ADR was provisional against:

| Criterion | Measured | |
|---|---|---|
| ≥65% of the golden set resolved at stages 1+2 | 30% | ❌ |
| Stage-3 top-1 ≥85% on the remainder, **1–1.5B model** | 86% on direct+paraphrase; 80% overall (`qwen2.5:1.5b`, topK 3) | ⚠️ |
| p95 < 2s end to end | 151ms | ✅ |

**The ADR is Accepted.** The cascade shape holds at 1B: `qwen2.5:1.5b` reaches 84–86%
top-1 on the buckets that resemble real traffic against the 8B's 90%, runs **6× faster**
end to end, and produces zero safety violations and zero hallucinated ids. The WP-01
stop-criterion (below ~70% on direct+paraphrase ⇒ redesign) was not triggered by either
1B-class model.

The 30% model-free rate remains below the 65% target. It is not a refutation of the
cascade: 35% of the golden set is deliberately un-resolvable before stage 3 (ambiguous +
out-of-scope + adversarial), and several capabilities carry required free-text params that
no deterministic resolver can fill. The gate needs a traffic-shaped denominator before it
means anything — tracked in FINDINGS Part I, "What the number actually means".

**Selected stage-3 model: `qwen2.5:1.5b` at `topK: 3`.** Not `gemma3:1b` — see below.

### Two mechanisms in this ADR do not survive a smaller model

Both were validated on the 8B and silently stop working at 1B. Neither invalidates the
cascade; both need replacing before MVP 1 ships.

**1 · The out-of-scope sentinel (Finding 7) — no working mechanism remains.**

The original text routed out-of-scope through a stage-2 `τ_floor` rejection. Finding 2
showed no encoder supports that and moved the judgement into stage 3 as an
`__out_of_scope__` value in the constrained enum. On the 8B that works (85–90%). It does
not transfer:

| | `llama3.1:8b` | `qwen2.5:1.5b` | `gemma3:1b` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Out-of-scope detected | 85% | 30% | **0%** |

`gemma3:1b` never selects the sentinel — zero times in 140 constrained calls, three runs
running. It answers "what is the capital of France" with `settings.currency` at **0.95
confidence**. Forced to choose from a menu, a small model chooses from the menu.

So: stage 2 cannot detect out-of-scope (Finding 2, both encoders) and stage 3 cannot at 1B.
**There is currently no out-of-scope detection on target hardware.** The layered design
still contains the damage — misroutes land overwhelmingly on `navigate`/`read`, and
adversarial safety held at 20/20 on every model — but containment is not detection.

*The replacement mechanism is deliberately NOT decided here.* Candidates are a fitted
stage-2 rejection classifier over (top score, margin, entropy) rather than a scalar
`τ_floor`; a dedicated binary in-domain classifier at stage 2.5; or accepting misroutes
and making recovery trivial in the UI. Choosing between them needs the dispatcher that
WP-03 builds. **A separate ADR must record that choice before MVP 1.**

**2 · The confidence gate (Finding 8) — still executing, no longer catching anything.**

`TAU_ACT_BY_KIND` assumes self-reported confidence is informative. Share of stage-3 calls
it held back: **15.7%** on the 8B, **0.7%** on `qwen2.5:1.5b`, **2.9%** on `gemma3:1b`.
Small models report high confidence nearly unconditionally, so the gate reads a near-
constant signal.

Worse, the degradation is invisible in the gate table — ambiguous→clarify reads 43.3% on
both the 8B and gemma, for entirely different reasons.

*Amendment: τ_act must stop depending on the model's self-report.* Prefer a signal the
model cannot inflate — stage-2 margin, or agreement between the stage-2 top candidate and
the stage-3 selection. Per-model calibration alone is insufficient: a confidence
distribution concentrated at 0.95 has no usable operating point.

### The generalisable lesson

The two things that broke are the two places this ADR delegated a *judgement* to the
model's own competence — "is this in scope?" and "how sure are you?". Everything
delegated to structure instead — the enum, the registry check, the policy table, the
deterministic-slot precedence — held on every model tested.

**A mechanism that depends on the model being good is not a mechanism.** Prefer structure
over model competence wherever the choice exists; where it does not, measure the mechanism
on the smallest model you intend to ship, not the largest one you have installed.
